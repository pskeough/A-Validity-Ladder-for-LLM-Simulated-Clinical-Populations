## 2. Methods as they should be written in the paper

**Estimand.** Level 2 asks whether a simulated gap between two groups matches the population gap. Every simulated contrast is conditional, because each persona pair differs on one attribute and matches on the rest. The persona design is a full factorial:
- race: Asian, Black, Hispanic, White, and Multiracial;
- gender: the two cisgender levels are used;
- income: three levels;
- relationship: Married or Single.

Each contrast is therefore an equal-weight average over the strata of the other attributes. Race contrasts have 12 strata (sex x income x relationship). The sex contrast has 24 (race x income x relationship, four races). Income contrasts have 16 (race x sex x relationship). We report the ratio of the simulated gap to two population references:
- The **standardised** gap is the NHANES contrast computed within the same strata and averaged with the same equal weights. It answers the question the design asks.
- The **marginal** gap is the crude NHANES difference between the two groups, the reference used in the earlier version. It answers whether the simulation reproduces the gap an analyst would see in the survey.

The two references can differ in sign. Standardised, the Black-White gap is -0.28 (SE 0.087); marginal, it is +0.33 (SE 0.083) [`l2_reference.csv`].

**Reference construction.** The reference is NHANES 2005-2018 (cycles D to J), adults with a complete PHQ-8, 36,274 respondents [`l2_reference_receipts.csv`]. Weight is WTMEC2YR/7. Coding:
- **Race:** RIDRETH1 gives White, Black and Hispanic (Mexican American and Other Hispanic). Asian is RIDRETH3 = 6, which exists only in 2011-2018.
- **Income:** INDFMPIR bands, Low below 1.3, Middle 1.3 to 3.5, High above 3.5. These are the published bands.
- **Relationship:** DMDMARTL. Married is married (1). Single is widowed, divorced, separated or never married (2 to 5), plus respondents aged 18 or 19 with no marital status. NHANES stopped asking 18 and 19 year olds from 2007, and in 2005-06, 91.4% of them were never married and 1.4% married. Living with a partner (6) is left out, since it is neither persona level.

Multiracial personas have no defensible NHANES counterpart. RIDRETH3 = 7 combines multiracial respondents with American Indian, Alaska Native and Pacific Islander respondents, exists only from 2011, and gives cells as small as 29. Multiracial pairs are therefore dropped from the sex and income contrasts on both sides.

Cell sizes: no standardisation cell is empty. The smallest primary cell holds 83 respondents for the sex, income and Asian contrasts, and 200 or more for the Black and Hispanic contrasts [`l2_reference.csv`, `min_cell_n`].

**Standard errors.** Population gaps use Taylor linearisation of the whole contrast, computed as a domain contrast so the covariance between the two groups is carried. The design has 105 strata and 214 PSUs, and design df is PSUs minus strata, 109 (108 for the Asian contrasts). A Rao-Wu rescaling bootstrap (2,000 replicates, seed 20260925) gives similar SEs and changes no verdict [`l2_reference.csv` `se_boot`; `l2_verdicts.csv` analysis `ref_se_bootstrap`].

Simulated gaps are computed as follows:
- A persona pair's difference is the difference of the two cell means (each over 30 draws), averaged over the two framings, so each pair counts once.
- A model's gap is the mean over its pairs, with SE sd/sqrt(n) and n-1 df.
- The pooled gap is the equal-weight mean of the four model gaps. Its SE treats the models as fixed strata, sqrt(sum se_m^2)/4, with Satterthwaite df.
- Pooled pairs: 48 for each race contrast, 96 for sex, 64 for each income contrast [`l2_report_table.csv`].

**Rule (R3).** The ratio rho = simulated gap / population gap is read against the boundaries -0.25, 0.25, 0.75 and 1.25, which define five regions: reversed, missing, attenuated, kept and steepened.
- At each boundary c we test rho > c and rho < c one-sided with t_c = (g - c p) / sqrt(se_g^2 + c^2 se_p^2), since the simulation and NHANES are independent samples.
- Degrees of freedom are Welch-Satterthwaite, combining the simulation df and the design df.
- The verdict names the region the interval lies in, or both names when it crosses one boundary, and is "undetermined" when it crosses more.
- The reported interval is the inversion of the same tests (the Fieller set).

**Multiplicity.** Each scope is a family of seven contrasts: the pooled panel, each model, and each framing within them in the framing analyses. Every one-sided test is run at .05/7 (Bonferroni), so the intervals are 98.6%. The unadjusted 90% reading is carried beside every verdict [`l2_verdicts.csv`, `verdict_unadjusted_90`].

**Stop rules.** Two preconditions are checked before R3 is read.
1. **No population gap.** The population gap's own family-level interval, p -/+ t(.05/7, df_p) se_p, covers zero.
2. **Reference too imprecise.** A simulation with no error, g exactly, would have a ratio interval of [g/p_hi, g/p_lo]. The stop fires when that interval spans three or more regions, the same width R3 calls undetermined. It fires only where R3 is undetermined anyway, and it names the reference as the cause.

A population-only version fires when k = t(.05/7, df_p) se_p / |p| exceeds 0.25, before the simulation is seen. The value 0.25 is the largest relative half-width at which every true ratio can be confined to two regions, since the narrowest bounded region, kept, spans a factor of 1.25/0.75. That version is reported in every row as `verdict_popstop` [`l2_stop.csv`].

## 5. External demos under R3

The same rule is applied to both demos, with the covariance between the two gaps in the test and the closed-form Fieller interval, and with the same stops [`l2_external_r3.csv`, `l2_external_r3_summary.csv`]. `paper_brm/external/RESULTS.md` still describes the level-2 readings under the old rule and is superseded on level 2 by these files. I did not edit it.

**OpinionQA (Meister et al. 2024).**
- **Reference type: marginal on both sides.** The model is steered on one attribute, and the Pew gap is the crude, unweighted difference between the two groups' respondents. Neither side is standardised over the other attributes.
- Family: each model x method x steering cell, with 8, 5 or 3 contrasts.
- The 154 published rows were 51 kept, 85 missing, 8 steepened, 1 reversed and 9 undetermined. Under R3 with Bonferroni they are 89 undetermined, 29 attenuated or kept, 17 missing or attenuated, 12 kept or steepened, 5 reversed or missing, 1 kept and 1 steepened.
- No row is stopped, and the population-only stop would stop 5.
- The published "missing" rows mostly become undetermined (48), missing or attenuated (17) or attenuated or kept (16) [`l2_external_r3_receipts.csv`].

**Argyle et al. 2023, Study 3.**
- **Reference type: the same respondents.** The silicon and human gaps are computed on identical people, so the two sides share one composition, and the comparison is free of the marginal-versus-standardised question. It is the cleanest level-2 case available.
- Family: one run, 61 contrasts.
- In the main run (temperature 0.7), 23 of 61 contrasts have no population gap at the family level and 12 are stopped as reference too imprecise [`l2_external_r3_summary.csv`, "Argyle by run"].
- The single-region readings in the main run:
  - Republican-Democrat Trump two-party share, kept, 0.92 [0.85, 0.99];

  - patriotism, Black-White (-0.08) and Republican-Democrat (-0.06), missing.

  All six were published as "missing".
- The within-outcome family (5 to 7 contrasts) is a sensitivity, and it gives more two-name verdicts, as expected.

## 6. Departures from the published analysis, and why

1. **Corpus.** v3, one invalid row dropped. The published level 2 ran on v2.
2. **Standardised reference added as co-primary.** The simulated contrasts are conditional by construction. The marginal reference compares them with a gap that also carries composition differences, and for the racial contrasts that difference reverses the sign of the NHANES gap.
3. **Relationship status enters the standardisation.** It is a persona attribute, so the conditional contrast holds it fixed. Cohabiting respondents are left out, and 18-19-year-olds with no marital status are treated as Single, with alternatives run as sensitivities.
4. **Four races, not five, for the sex and income contrasts.** Multiracial has no NHANES counterpart (section 2). Pooled pairs are 96 for sex and 64 for each income contrast. The published counts, 240 and 160, counted each framing separately and included Multiracial.
5. **Pair-clustered, model-stratified SEs.** Script 66 treated the clinical and narrative versions of one persona pair as independent units. The two are correlated, at 0.14 to 0.52 within model and 0.26 to 0.90 pooled [`l2_sim_receipts.csv`], so each pair now counts once.
6. **Population SE as a domain contrast.** The published SE was hypot(se_a, se_b), which drops the covariance of the two groups within PSUs. For Women-Men it gave 0.066 against 0.048 [`l2_reference.csv`].
7. **Degrees of freedom.** Welch-Satterthwaite from the simulation df and the design df (109), in place of the pairs' df for both terms.
8. **Rule.** R3 replaces script 66's sequence of equivalence tests, as adopted on 25 Sep (`level2_rule/LEVEL2_RULE.md`). "Attenuated" replaces "flattened", which collides with Wang et al.'s "group flattening".
9. **Multiplicity.** Bonferroni within each scope, including each model, at .05/7. The published analysis used BH q across the seven pooled contrasts and raw p per model, so per-model verdicts had no multiplicity control.
10. **Stops.** "No population gap" and "reference too imprecise" are new, with the conditional version as primary (section 7, question 1).
11. **Prompt control.** It is analysed on the complete log (two GLM-4.7 cells gain a draw) and on both references, with Bonferroni over five contrasts.
12. **External demos.** R3 with the gap covariance and Bonferroni families replaces the old rule with BH q.
