## 2. Methods as they should read in the paper

**Estimand.** The simulated population is a balanced factorial of personas, and each NHANES group has its own composition. Level 3 compares the two at the population's composition. For a group G (all adults, one race, one sex or one income band), each persona cell c in G (race by sex by income band by relationship status) receives the weight p_c = N_c / sum over c' in G of N_c', where N_c is the NHANES weighted count of adults in cell c. The simulated level is sum_c p_c s_c, where s_c is the persona's mean PHQ-8 over draws, and the reference is the NHANES weighted mean over the same classified adults, sum_c p_c y_c. The residual is R_G = sum_c p_c (s_c - y_c). Forty-eight of the 120 personas can be matched: transgender personas have no reference (NHANES records sex at interview), and multiracial personas have none (RIDRETH3 category 7 merges multiracial with American Indian, Alaska Native and Pacific Islander respondents and exists only from 2011). Relationship follows the level-2 coding: married against widowed, divorced, separated or never married, cohabiting adults excluded, 18-19-year-olds not asked counted single. The classified population is 85.9% of weighted adults in the four races, and the four races are 95.7% of weighted adults [80a_coverage.csv]. Asian adults are identifiable only from 2011, so in multi-cycle windows their weights are divided by the number of cycles that identify them. Personas carry no age, so the simulated population's age mix is unknown and unmatched. The marginal comparison of the published version (the equal-weight persona mean against the NHANES group mean) is reported as a secondary estimand and labelled as such.

**Standard errors.** The persona cells are fixed by design, so the simulated side's only sampling error is the draw sampling within a cell: var(s_cf) = S2_cf / n_cf for framing f, with n_cf draws (30 in the full corpus). The combined framing averages the two framing means, var = (var_clin + var_narr) / 4, and the pooled panel averages the four models, var = sum_m var_m / 16. For the post-stratified residual, var_sim(R_G) = sum_c p_c^2 var(s_c). The reference side is estimated by the stratified delete-one-PSU jackknife on the NHANES design (strata SDMVSTRA, PSUs SDMVPSU, MEC weights): var_ref(R_G) = sum_h (n_h - 1) / n_h sum_j (R_G(hj) - R_G)^2, with the persona values held fixed. This carries the sampling error of the NHANES cell means and of the composition weights together, and it treats each group as a domain of the full sample. Taylor linearisation of the same contrast agrees to within 0.03% [80c_taylor_vs_jk.csv]. The two sides are independent, so SE = sqrt(var_sim + var_ref). Degrees of freedom follow Satterthwaite with the design degrees of freedom (PSUs minus strata: 109 for 2005-2018, 25 for 2017-2020, 15 for 2021-2023) on the reference term [80a_tolerance_basis.csv]. The published SE, the spread of cell means across the design divided by the square root of the number of cells, treats the fixed design factors as sampling noise.

**Tolerances.** A tolerance is the smallest effect of interest for the stated use (Lakens, Scheel & Isager, 2018). For a mean-level use, three bands are reported: 0.2 NHANES SD (0.79 points; SD 3.94 in adults 2005-2018), the conventional small standardised difference; 1 point, about the size of the population sex gap and of the change in the population mean between 2005-2018 and 2021-2023 (2.98 to 3.97); and 2 points, about 0.5 SD and the size of the low-to-high income gap [80a_tolerance_basis.csv]. On the NHANES distribution a uniform shift of 1 point raises the prevalence of PHQ-8 >= 10 by 1.73 percentage points and a shift of 2 points by 4.04 [80a_tolerance_basis.csv], so the 2-point band already allows the population prevalence to rise by more than half. The 5-point band, the PHQ-9 minimal clinically important change (Löwe et al., 2004), is a within-person change threshold and is reported only as an individual-level benchmark. For a screening or prevalence use, the share at or above 10 (the PHQ-8 cut point; Kroenke et al., 2009) is compared as a difference within 2.5 and 5 percentage points and as a ratio within 0.80 to 1.25, the bioequivalence convention. The anchor's own interval is not a tolerance and is reported instead as the test's resolution, 1.645 SE at a zero residual.

**Pass rule.** A row passes at tolerance delta when two one-sided tests at alpha .05 reject both boundaries, that is, when the 90% interval of R_G lies inside (-delta, +delta) (Schuirmann, 1987). The prevalence ratio is tested with two linear one-sided contrasts (s - 0.80 y > 0 and s - 1.25 y < 0) and reported with a Fieller interval, because a log-scale delta method fails when the simulated prevalence is near zero. A model passes level 3 for a use at delta only when all ten group rows pass. This is an intersection-union test and holds its size at alpha with no multiplicity adjustment (Berger, 1982). A use that needs only the population total reads the overall row. Pooled rows describe the panel average and are secondary to the per-model verdicts.

**What a pass licenses.** A pass at delta says that, for this prompt, this persona grid and this NHANES era, the model's simulated population, reweighted to the real composition of each group, has a mean PHQ-8 within delta of the real group's. It licenses using the panel's level as a stand-in for the population's level within delta. It does not license prevalence or screening uses (the prevalence test is separate, and the two disagree in 8 of 50 primary rows), comparisons between groups (level 2), single-draw labels (the gate), or any age-specific population.

**Sensitivities.** Every verdict is also reported on the 2017-2020 pre-pandemic file and the 2021-2023 cycle (each with its own weights, strata and PSUs, never pooled across designs), on persona-string income anchors (2007-2018, the cycles with the $100,000 top code), on two other marital codings, per framing and on the December-only corpus (clinical rows from `dec28_main` and `dec28_restored`, all narrative rows).

## 3. Departures from the published analysis

- Primary estimand is post-stratified; the published marginal comparison becomes a labelled secondary estimand. A design-standardised variant (equal weight over the group's persona cells on both sides, the level-2 reference) is also computed (`estimand == "DS"` in 80c_l3_results.csv).
- Simulated SE is draw sampling within cells, not the spread of cell means. Reference SE is a domain jackknife that includes composition uncertainty. Degrees of freedom are Satterthwaite on the design df, not cells minus one.
- The strict tolerance is dropped. The 5-point band is demoted to an individual benchmark. Three mean tolerances (0.2 SD, 1 point, 2 points) and three prevalence tolerances are added.
- Prevalence of PHQ-8 >= 10 is added as a second level-3 outcome.
- The unit is reported per framing and combined; the published version pooled the 60 rows of both framings, which equals the combined reading when both framings have 30 draws.
- Multiracial personas no longer enter the sex and income marginals of the primary estimand (they did in the published version). The 48 matched personas are used throughout.
- Corpus v3 replaces v2: 43 December GPT-4o-mini rows restored, the invalid DeepSeek row dropped rather than clipped.
- The 2021-2023 and 2017-2020 anchors are restored as sensitivities. Persona-string income anchors are added.
- Asian weights in multi-cycle windows are divided by four, the number of cycles that identify Asian adults. This changes only the composition weights, not any single-race mean.
- Low income is PIR < 1.3 (script 03). Scripts 26 and 30 used <= 1.30; the difference is 0.024 points on the 2021-2023 low-income anchor and less elsewhere [80a_gates.csv].
- A model passes only if every group passes (intersection-union), and the published count of passing cells is replaced by per-model verdicts.
