## 1. What level 1 now says

No model passes level 1 in either framing. Read against people rather than against random routing, every model is more prototypical than real respondents, and the published reading reverses: GLM-4.7, which the paper removed from every use above the single case, is the model closest to people.

The failure has one shape in all four models. At the same PHQ-8 total, model draws land in neither tail of the human person-fit distribution. People put 5% of vectors below their own 5th percentile of lz* and 5% above their 95th. The models put 0.0% to 1.0% below and 0.0% to 4.3% above. The models produce fewer atypical presentations than people, and also fewer highly regular ones. The report calls this "compressed". The item profile shows where the typicality comes from. At matched totals the models put load on depressed mood, anhedonia and worthlessness and take it off sleep and fatigue, which are the items people endorse first. A textbook presentation is therefore not the same as a population-typical one.

Ranked by distance from people on person fit (rule stated in `79f` before the ranks were read). The rank is the same in both framings, and no verdict differs between clinical and narrative framing for any model.

1. **GLM-4.7. Fails, misfit deficit only.** Its overfit tail is equivalent to people's (3.9% of draws above the NHANES 95th, ratio 90% CI 0.67 to 1.03 pooled over framings; clinical 0.55 to 0.92, narrative 0.74 to 1.15). It produces almost no misfitting vectors (0.1%). Gateway O/E is 0.21: at the same totals it violates the rule at about a fifth of the human rate. Cross-persona pattern reuse is 4.6 to 4.9 times the human rate.
2. **GPT-4o-mini. Fails, compressed.** 0.9% misfit and 0.0% overfit. Its vectors sit below people's in the middle of the distribution (mean mid-PIT 0.38), so its patterns are less typical than people's at the same total but never extreme. Gateway O/E 0.07. Reuse 5.3 to 6.0 times.
3. **Gemini-3-Flash. Fails, compressed.** 0.0% misfit and 0.9% overfit, sitting above people in the middle (mid-PIT 0.66). Gateway O/E 0.02. Reuse 22 to 39 times.
4. **DeepSeek-V3. Fails, compressed.** 0.2% misfit and 0.0% overfit, mid-PIT 0.37. Gateway O/E 0.009. Reuse 30 to 35 times.

The verdicts hold at every tolerance from 1.10 to 3.00, on December-only rows, under the unweighted GRM, and (with one softening, section 5) against NHANES 2021-2023.

The NHANES gateway rate has a linearised design SE of 0.98 points (bootstrap 1.02) [`l1_gateway_nhanes.csv`]. Pass bands at tau = 1.5: O/E and tail ratios inside 0.667 to 1.5. The reuse ratio is the probability that two draws from different personas at the same total are the identical vector, divided by the same probability for two NHANES adults at that total [`l1_pattern_reuse.csv`].

What the planted controls show [`l1_planted_controls.csv`]. The corpus design is kept (same rows, personas and totals) and the vectors are replaced. An NHANES adult with the same total passes both statistics (misfit ratio 1.02, overfit 1.03, gateway O/E 1.01). A uniform random composition of the total reads excess misfit on person fit (ratio 6.05). The same random vectors read "too prototypical" on the gateway (O/E 0.62), because random routing spreads load evenly while people put it on the somatic items first. The single most regular vector at each total reads excess overfit (19.3) and passes the gateway (O/E 1.21). Textbook routing (a real respondent's values with the two largest moved to items 1 and 2) reads too prototypical on the gateway (O/E 0) and excess misfit on person fit (2.63). The gateway cannot tell random routing from stereotyping, so it cannot carry a verdict alone. Person fit separates all four controls.

## 2. Methods as they should be written

**Reference sample.** Adults aged 18 and over in NHANES 2005-2018 who answered all eight PHQ-9 items that make up the PHQ-8 (DPQ010 to DPQ080) with a valid response of 0 to 3 and have a positive examination weight (n = 36,274; 3,070 with a total of 10 or more) [`l1_nhanes_sample_flow.csv`, `l1_gateway_nhanes.csv`]. Estimates use WTMEC2YR / 7 with the masked design variables SDMVSTRA and SDMVPSU. NHANES 2021-2023 (n = 5,457) is a sensitivity reference.

**Calibration model.** A graded response model (Samejima, 1969) with a standard normal trait was fitted to the reference sample by marginal maximum likelihood (81-point quadrature from -6 to 6). The survey weights enter as a pseudo-likelihood rescaled to sum to n [`l1_grm_params.csv`]. The model-implied total-score distribution departs from the weighted observed one by a total-variation distance of 0.033 [`l1_grm_fit_checks.csv`, `l1_grm_total_fit.csv`]. Every NHANES respondent and every generated draw is scored with these item parameters, so the population defines the metric.

**Person-fit statistic.** For a vector x with trait estimate theta-hat, the polytomous log-likelihood statistic of Drasgow, Levine and Williams (1985) is

l0 = sum_j sum_k d_jk log P_jk(theta), where d_jk = 1 if x_j = k,

with P_jk the GRM category probability. It is standardised with the correction for an estimated trait (Snijders, 2001), in the polytomous form of Sinharay (2016):

lz* = [ sum_j sum_k (d_jk - P_jk) w_jk + c_n r_0 ] / sqrt( sum_j Var_j(w~_j) ),

where w_jk = log P_jk, r_jk = P'_jk / P_jk, c_n = (sum_jk P'_jk w_jk) / (sum_jk P'_jk r_jk), w~_jk = w_jk - c_n r_jk, and Var_j(w~_j) = sum_k P_jk w~_jk^2 - (sum_k P_jk w~_jk)^2, all evaluated at theta-hat. Theta-hat is Warm's (1989) weighted likelihood estimate, which is finite for the all-zero vector, and r_0 = J / (2I) is its bias term. On vectors simulated from the fitted model at theta from 0.5 to 2.5, lz* has SD 0.95 to 0.98 while uncorrected lz has SD 0.60 to 0.88 [`l1_personfit_calibration.csv`]. Low lz* marks a vector less likely than the model expects at its trait level (misfit). High lz* marks one more likely than expected (overfit: too regular).

**Matching on total.** lz* depends on the total, and the models' totals are not distributed like people's. So each draw is placed in the NHANES weighted distribution of lz* among respondents with the same total. Totals are used exactly from 0 to 19, and 20-21 and 22-24 are pooled so that every stratum holds at least 100 respondents [`l1_personfit_nhanes_ref.csv`]. Ties are split by mass, which gives the exact expectation of a randomised probability integral transform. The reference therefore puts exactly 5% below its own within-total 5th percentile and 5% above its 95th, at any total mix. The misfit ratio is a model's share below the 5th percentile divided by 0.05, and the overfit ratio is its share above the 95th divided by 0.05. The unmatched shares (NHANES percentiles over all adults) are reported alongside [`l1_personfit_main.csv`, columns `marg_*`]. They mix pattern with level and are not read.

**Tolerance.** The equivalence band is 1/tau to tau on each ratio. tau is the smallest value in {1.10, 1.25, 1.50, 2.00, 3.00} that contains the point ratio of every NHANES subgroup (sex, race and ethnicity, poverty-income band, survey cycle, and the 2021-2023 cycle) scored against the pooled reference. The principle is that the population's own subgroups must pass. The largest subgroup departure is 1.36 (non-Hispanic Black adults, misfit tail), so tau = 1.5 [`l1_personfit_tolerance.csv`]. With their intervals, all 19 subgroups pass person fit at tau = 1.5. The same rule on the gateway O/E gives a largest departure of 1.31 (cycle 2005-2006, O/E 0.76) and also tau = 1.5. There 18 of 19 subgroups read equivalent, and cycle 2005-2006 reads "too prototypical or equivalent" [`l1_gateway_tolerance.csv`]. No model's verdict changes at any tau from 1.10 to 3.00 [`l1_personfit_verdicts_tau.csv`, `l1_gateway_verdicts_tau.csv`].

**Pass rule.** A model passes level 1 in a framing when the 90% intervals of both its misfit ratio and its overfit ratio lie inside [1/tau, tau]. Each tail is read on the interval, in the manner of the level-2 rule: above the band, below it, inside it, crossing one boundary (two names), or undetermined. Excess misfit means less coherent than people. A misfit deficit means fewer atypical vectors than people. Excess overfit means too regular. An overfit deficit means fewer regular vectors. A deficit in both tails is "compressed". A pass needs both tails, so it is an intersection-union test and needs no multiplicity adjustment (Berger, 1982). Intervals come from a persona-clustered bootstrap of the corpus (a persona is a profile within a model; both framings of a persona move together when framings are pooled), drawn jointly with a Rao-Wu bootstrap over NHANES PSUs within strata (B = 2000).

**The gateway row (descriptive).** An elevated case (total of 10 or more) violates the gateway rule when items 1 and 2 are both below 2. The rate falls from 54% of NHANES cases at a total of 10 to 7% at 17-18, and it cannot occur above 20 [`l1_gateway_nhanes_by_total.csv`]. So it is compared at matched totals. E is the NHANES violation rate at each case's own total band, averaged over the model's elevated cases, and O/E is read against the same band as person fit. Direct standardisation to the NHANES total mix on common support gives the same ordering [`l1_gateway_main.csv`, `direct_std_ratio`]. The row is descriptive because random routing reads "too prototypical" on it (section 1).

**Unit.** Level 1 is scored on single draws, with inference clustered on persona. A draw is one simulated respondent's answer vector, and level 1 asks whether that vector is one a person could plausibly give. A cell mean is not such a vector: none of the 960 cell-mean vectors has integer items. Applying the gateway rule to cell means turns 64.9% of elevated cells into violations, against 2.7% of elevated draws, because averaging pulls items 1 and 2 below 2 [`l1_unit_cellmeans.csv`]. The cell mean remains the unit for levels 2 and 3, which ask about expected answers, not about individual vectors.

**What a pass licenses.** A pass licenses using single draws as individual cases whose item pattern is as plausible, and as varied, as a real respondent's at the same severity. Examples are exemplar vignettes, item-level test cases for screening tools, and training data for classifiers that read item patterns. A pass says nothing about levels or group differences. A compressed failure rules out uses that depend on the spread of presentations: stress-testing a screener with atypical cases, estimating how often presentations are atypical, or training a model to recognise non-textbook depression.

## 3. Departures from the published analysis

1. **Reference.** The published comparator was a random-routing null. It is replaced by NHANES, because beating random routing says nothing about fidelity to how people present. Under the published rule (observed under half the model's own null), three models pass and GLM-4.7 fails. Against people, all four fail, and GLM-4.7 is closest.
2. **Two-sided.** Too few violations, and too few atypical vectors, are failures, not passes.
3. **Main statistic.** GRM person fit (lz*) replaces the single gateway rule. The gateway becomes a descriptive row.
4. **Matched on total.** Gateway rates and person-fit tails are compared at the same total. Unmatched, the pooled gateway contrast is 2.69% against 28.79%. At the models' own totals, people would violate at 34.3%.
5. **The null as coded (audit B5).** Script 22 pooled elevated cases per model and profile across both framings, drew marginals from elevated cases only, and conditioned on the total by rejection with 60 retries. On v3 that construction gives 10.44% pooled (published 10.42%). The null as the paper describes it (each case's own model x profile x framing cell, marginals from all 30 draws, exact conditional probability) gives 10.57% pooled. It moves GLM-4.7 from 6.12% to 8.04%, so GLM-4.7's observed 6.24% is now below its own null (observed / null 0.78, 95% CI 0.69 to 0.86), though not below half of it. The other models are DeepSeek-V3 13.01%, Gemini-3-Flash 6.28% and GPT-4o-mini 19.33%, and no verdict under the published rule changes [`l1_gateway_null.csv`]. If the null is kept in the paper, it should be this corrected version, as a descriptive row.
6. **Corpus v3.** 9,598 elevated rows against the published 9,590. GPT-4o-mini is at 3.36% against the published 3.32% (the 43 restored December rows). The pooled rate is 2.69% against 2.68% [`l1_gateway_null.csv`, `published_observed`].
7. **Intervals.** Every rate and ratio has a persona-clustered interval. The published level 1 reported none.
8. **Gender identity (audit C17).** Transgender personas supply 6,979 of the 9,598 elevated draws [`l1_gateway_main.csv`, subset rows]. Level 1 is now also reported separately for cisgender and transgender personas. The verdicts are the same in both, with one difference: in GLM-4.7's cisgender personas the overfit tail is equivalent (5.4% of draws, ratio 90% CI 0.92 to 1.36) and only the misfit deficit remains [`l1_personfit_main.csv`]. GLM-4.7's gateway rate is 13.1% in cisgender personas and 4.0% in transgender personas [`l1_gateway_main.csv`].

## 4. Person fit and the total score

In NHANES, lz* barely tracks the total (Spearman 0.08). In the models it falls with the total, most in Gemini-3-Flash (Spearman -0.73), and in DeepSeek-V3 (-0.09), GPT-4o-mini (-0.14) and GLM-4.7 (-0.20) [`l1_personfit_by_band.csv`]. The compressed pattern holds within every PHQ band where a model has draws. In the 5-9 band, no model reaches 1% below the matched 5th percentile, and the overfit share runs from 0.0% (DeepSeek-V3) to 4.6% (GLM-4.7). In the 10-14 band, no model exceeds 2.0% in either tail; the largest is GPT-4o-mini's misfit share at 2.0%. At matched totals the models score 0.16 to 0.51 points higher than people on depressed mood and 0.01 to 0.29 higher on anhedonia. They score 0.22 to 1.11 points lower on fatigue and 0.02 to 0.47 lower on appetite [`l1_personfit_item_profile.csv`]. On sleep, three models score lower (-0.35 to -0.44) and GLM-4.7 is 0.10 higher. No model produces flat vectors (all eight items equal): 0 to 0.01% of draws, against 0.4% to 1.9% of NHANES respondents at the same totals.

## 5. Sensitivities

- **December-only rows** (clinical rows from `dec28_main` or `dec28_restored`, all narrative rows): every person-fit and gateway verdict is unchanged [`l1_personfit_main.csv`, `l1_gateway_main.csv`, sample `dec_only`; `l1_summary.csv`].
- **NHANES 2021-2023 as the reference** (same item parameters): the NHANES gateway rate is 33.0% [`l1_gateway_nhanes.csv`] and every gateway verdict is unchanged. On person fit, Gemini-3-Flash and GLM-4.7 are unchanged. DeepSeek-V3 and GPT-4o-mini move from compressed to "overfit deficit", because their misfit tail widens to undetermined (GPT-4o-mini misfit ratio 0.64 to 1.84, DeepSeek-V3 0.16 to 0.98). The 2021-2023 cycle has 5,457 respondents against 36,274, so its within-total tails are much less precise. Its own overfit ratio against the 2005-2018 reference is 1.22 [`l1_personfit_tolerance.csv`], so its lz* distribution sits higher.
- **Unweighted GRM**: every verdict is unchanged [`l1_personfit_main.csv`, reference `2005_2018 unweighted GRM`].
