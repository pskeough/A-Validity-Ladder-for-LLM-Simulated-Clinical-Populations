## 2. Methods as they should read in the paper

**Unit of analysis.** A draw is one simulated respondent. A model's simulated population in one framing is every valid draw across its 120 personas (30 draws each, 3,600 draws; 3,599 for DeepSeek-V3 clinical). Its item covariance is the analogue of the NHANES population covariance: both mix variation between demographic profiles with variation among respondents who share a profile. The persona is the independent unit for inference, because its 30 draws share one prompt; every interval resamples personas, never draws. We do not treat within-persona covariance as replicate noise. A persona fixes sex, race, income and relationship status, the same four attributes that define a NHANES demographic cell. Variation among draws of one persona is therefore the model's counterpart of variation among people who share a cell, and in NHANES that is where the PHQ-8's general factor lives (96.5% of item variance, and a within-cell lambda1/lambda2 of 4.33). A simulated population that reproduces the NHANES correlation matrix only through differences between personas has a different structure, and rule R3 tests for it. Pooled rows (four models together, or one model over both framings) are description and carry no verdict.

**Correlations.** Polychoric correlations by two-step maximum likelihood (Olsson, 1979). Thresholds come from the weighted marginal proportions, tau_ik = Phi^-1(P(X_i <= k)), and each rho_ij maximises sum over a, b of n_ab log pi_ab(rho), where pi_ab is the bivariate normal probability of the rectangle (tau_i,a-1, tau_i,a] x (tau_j,b-1, tau_j,b]. NHANES tables use MEC weights. Pearson matrices are computed alongside and reported [l4_polychoric.csv].

**Dimensionality and the general factor.** One-factor model by unweighted least squares (minres) on the polychoric matrix: minimise sum over i < j of (r_ij - lambda_i lambda_j)^2. Report the eigenvalues, lambda1/lambda2, polychoric parallel analysis (100 column permutations, which keep every item's marginal distribution; factors retained while the observed eigenvalue exceeds the 95th percentile of its permutation counterpart; Horn, 1965), SRMR and omega total, omega = (sum lambda_i)^2 / [(sum lambda_i)^2 + sum (1 - lambda_i^2)]. **R1** passes when every loading is at least .30 and lambda1/lambda2 is at least 3. NHANES has lambda1/lambda2 7.47 and a smallest loading of .67, so the rule is far inside what people show. A population without a general factor fails level 4; its later rules are reported as description.

**Loading congruence.** Tucker's phi between the model's loadings lambda and the NHANES loadings mu: phi = sum lambda_i mu_i / sqrt(sum lambda_i^2 sum mu_i^2). The interval pairs replicate b of a persona bootstrap of the model (1,000 replicates) with replicate b of a Rao-Wu bootstrap of NHANES (Rao & Wu, 1988; n_h - 1 PSUs drawn with replacement in each stratum, weights scaled by n_h/(n_h - 1)), so both sources' sampling error enter. **R2** passes when the 5th percentile of the paired replicates is at least .95 (Lorenzo-Seva & ten Berge, 2006). As calibration, phi between the NHANES loadings and a flat vector is reported; it is .998.

**Source of the factor.** Decompose each population's item covariance into a between-cell part and a pooled within-cell part, S_T = S_B + S_W, where for the corpus the cell is the persona and for NHANES the cell is sex by race (White, Black, Asian, Hispanic, other) by income band by relationship status. The between share of item i is S_B,ii / S_T,ii. Standardise S_W to a correlation matrix and fit the one-factor model. **R3** passes when that within-cell matrix clears the R1 rule. Intervals resample personas (500 replicates) or NHANES PSUs within strata. NHANES is also run on finer cells (adding a four-level age band and survey cycle) to show that its within-cell factor does not depend on cell size. This decomposition uses Pearson covariances.

**Invariance.** One common factor for the eight items, fitted by multi-group normal-theory maximum likelihood with a mean structure. Configural: loadings, residual variances and intercepts free in every group, factor variance 1, factor mean 0. Metric: loadings equal across groups, factor variance free in groups 2 to G. Scalar: loadings and intercepts equal, factor means free in groups 2 to G. The fit function is

F = sum_g (n_g / N) [ ln|Sigma_g| + tr(S_g Sigma_g^-1) - ln|S_g| - p + (m_g - mu_g)' Sigma_g^-1 (m_g - mu_g) ],

with weighted moments for NHANES. Groups are sex (women, men; cisgender personas only in the corpus), race (White, Black, Asian, Hispanic) and income (low, middle, high; NHANES poverty-income ratio below 1.3, 1.3 to 3.5, above 3.5). The change in fit between nested steps is read as the RMSEA of the difference (Savalei, Brace & Fouladi, 2024), in its multi-group form,

RMSEA_D = sqrt(G) sqrt( max(0, dF - nu) / d ),

where dF is the difference in F, d the difference in degrees of freedom (7 per extra group at each step here: metric d = 7(G - 1), scalar d = 7(G - 1)), and nu the value dF takes under exact invariance. Normal theory gives nu = d/N. The draws are clustered in personas and the items are skewed and coarse, so nu is estimated as the mean dF over 200 permutations of group labels among independent units (personas; NHANES respondents within PSU), following Jorgensen, Kite, Chen and Short (2018). The permutation nu is 1.6 to 5.2 times the normal-theory value in NHANES and 2.1 to 13.7 times it in the corpus, so the uncorrected statistic would overstate non-invariance [l4_invariance.csv, column c_hat]. The 90% interval comes from 200 cluster-bootstrap replicates (personas resampled within group; NHANES Rao-Wu), recentred on the corrected estimate. At margin e0 a step holds when the upper 90% limit is below e0, fails when the lower limit is above e0, and is otherwise indeterminate. This is the equivalence reading of Yuan and Chan (2016) and Marcoulides and Yuan (2017), with the upper limit as a one-sided 5% test. The primary margin is .05 ("close fit"; MacCallum, Browne & Sugawara, 1996); .08 is reported as a sensitivity. The metric step is refitted on each group's polychoric matrix as an ordinal robustness check. Constrained models are refitted from the less constrained solution, so dF is never negative through a local minimum. The configural fit was checked against semopy 2.3 fitted group by group (relative difference 1e-6) [l4_semopy_gate.csv].

**R4** compares each model step with the same NHANES step at the same margin. A step matches when both verdicts are the same and determinate, mismatches when both are determinate and differ, and is unresolved otherwise. R4 passes when all six steps (metric and scalar for three attributes) match and fails when any step mismatches. A model without a general factor gets no R4 verdict.

**What a pass would license.** Passing R1 and R2 licenses scoring the model's draws as a one-dimensional PHQ-8 with human-like item weights, at the level of the whole simulated population. Passing R3 adds that the structure holds among draws of one persona, which is what licenses treating draws as individual respondents (item-level simulation, reliability or factor analyses on simulated respondents). Passing R4 adds that group comparisons of the total score mean the same thing in the model as in people. No model reaches R3, so no current model licenses individual-level psychometric use, and the two models with a general factor license the population-level item weighting only.

**Secondary statistics.** The within-model group-versus-group congruence of the published analysis is reported with persona bootstrap intervals (200 replicates) [l4_group_congruence.csv]. The severity-matched Frobenius distance of scripts 68 and 73 is recomputed with a permutation null and a size-free cross-fitted distance [l4_floor_null.csv]; section 1, item 7, gives the reason it carries no verdict.

## 3. Departures from the published analysis

- Per model and per framing, not pooled over models. The pooled income congruence of 0.70 is a pooling artefact (item 6).
- A population with no general factor now fails level 4 outright. The published analysis compared groups within populations that had no factor to compare.
- Draw-level correlations are compared with NHANES person-level correlations on the same footing, with the persona as the resampling unit. The published comparison set draw-level corpus correlations against person-level NHANES ones through floors built on different units.
- Polychoric correlations, not Pearson, for the structure and congruence statistics. Pearson results are carried beside them and agree on every R1 verdict [l4_structure.csv, pearson_general_factor].
- A new rule, R3, on where the factor comes from.
- Invariance by multi-group CFA with equivalence tests on RMSEA_D, replacing congruence as the test of group equivalence. Congruence is kept as description.
- The floor multiple and its 0.5 to 1.4 band are dropped (item 7). The permutation test beside it, whether the observed matched distance exceeds the 95th percentile of the null, is kept as description [l4_floor_null.csv, above_null].
- Corpus v3 (28,799 valid rows), NHANES 2005-2018 with MEC weights and design-based bootstraps.

## 5. What was not done

- No ordinal scalar invariance (see question 5).
- No partial invariance search. The income failures are large enough that the question of which items drive them is a description task, not a verdict task.
- The cross-fitted size-free distance in 81d is too noisy at the corpus's persona counts to carry anything: for sex and race its 95% intervals span zero in every model row, and in NHANES it is not severity-matched. It is in the CSV and not in the argument [l4_floor_null.csv].
