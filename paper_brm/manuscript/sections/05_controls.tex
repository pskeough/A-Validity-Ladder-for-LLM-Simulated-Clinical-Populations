\section{Controls: Real People and Planted Failures}
\label{sec:controls}

With every model failing every rung above the gate, the ladder itself has to be shown to leave real people unfailed and to catch failures of a known size.
The controls build pseudo-models out of real survey respondents.
Each replicate splits the NHANES adults at random into two halves within the 48 persona cells, uses one half as the reference, and fills the corpus design from the other half, with 120 personas, two framings and 30 draws, where each draw is a real respondent from the persona's cell.
A panel of four such pseudo-models stands in for the four language models.
Failures are then planted into the same respondents, each aimed at one rung, by making the items independent, shifting every persona mean, doubling the income gap, compressing answer patterns toward the typical one, and making three items independent within the low-income group.
Run on the real corpus with the full reference, the control code reproduces all 303 published rung numbers. % R: 83a_receipt.csv

\paragraph{Real people are rarely failed, and planted failures are caught.}
Real respondents pass the gate and level 3 in every reading, level 1 in 92.9\% of replicates, and R1 and R2 of level 4 in every replicate, and level 2 fails them in no reading (Table~\ref{tab:panel}). % R: analysis_brm/intermediate_panel/88_confusion.csv
At 30 draws real respondents' persona means reach a dependability of only .70, because real demographic cells differ little from one another. % R: analysis_brm/intermediate_panel/88_panel_verdicts.csv
Applied to every study, a floor of $\phi \ge .80$ would fail real people, and the gate therefore requires it only of studies that compare or rank individual personas.
Each planted failure trips the rung it targets. % R: analysis_brm/intermediate_panel/88_confusion.csv
Independent items fail level 1, R1 and R2 in every replicate, and compressed samples fail level 1 in every replicate. % R: analysis_brm/intermediate_panel/88_confusion.csv
Shifted samples fail level 3 in every reading, and non-invariant samples fail R4 in all 25 replicates. % R: analysis_brm/intermediate_panel/88_confusion.csv
A doubled income gap fails level 2 in 92.5\% of per-model readings. % R: analysis_brm/intermediate_panel/88_confusion.csv
Two rungs also react to failures they do not target.
A sample shifted by 2.1 points passes the gate in only 28.6\% of readings, as more depressed respondents vary more between draws. % R: analysis_brm/intermediate_panel/88_confusion.csv
R2 also fails compressed and non-invariant samples, both of which change the size of the loadings.
Over a further 200 replicates with graded doses, each rung detects in 95\% of replicates a distortion smaller than the one the language models show at levels 1, 3 and 4 (Supplement S5).
In those replicates real respondents' misfit ratio averages 1.15, and level 1 passes them at $\tau = 1.5$ in 99.4\% of 800 per-model readings, which sets the level-1 floor. % R: 83c_l1.csv

\paragraph{What level 2 and R4 need to certify a pass.}
A group gap is \vd{kept} when the whole interval for the ratio of the simulated gap to the real gap lies between 0.75 and 1.25, placing the simulated gap within a quarter of the real gap's size.
Because the whole interval must fall inside that range, a kept verdict depends on the precision of both gap estimates as well as on how close they are.
Certifying a kept gap therefore needs more data than detecting a gap that is not kept, and level 2 and the invariance rule R4 both carry this asymmetry.
A second simulation repeated the control panel over 60 replicates on grids of 48, 144 and 432 persona cells. % R: analysis_brm/panel_power/90_cells.csv; analysis_brm/panel_power/90b_summary.csv
Level 2 fails a faithful simulator built from real respondents in at most 3.3\% of per-model readings and fails a doubled income gap in 97.5\% to 100\%. % R: analysis_brm/panel_power/90b_summary.csv
At 30 draws per persona the low-to-high income gap of a faithful simulator is never read \vd{kept}, because draws of one persona vary enough to hold its interval wider than the kept region. % R: analysis_brm/panel_power/power_l2_contrast_readings.csv
With no simulation error, the limit of adding draws, the same gap is read \vd{kept} in 95.0\% and 98.3\% of replicates on the 48-cell and 144-cell grids. % R: analysis_brm/panel_power/power_l2_contrast_readings.csv; analysis_brm/panel_power/90_cells.csv
The Black--White and Hispanic--White gaps are never read \vd{kept} on any grid, because NHANES measures them too loosely. % R: analysis_brm/panel_power/power_l2_contrast_readings.csv
A researcher who needs a kept gap therefore plans for more draws per persona and for a population gap the survey estimates with a narrow interval, and a finer persona grid does not supply the second.
R4 shows the same asymmetry, and at the .08 margin, 24 to 40 personas per group leave some invariance steps unresolved for real respondents in 24 of 25 replicates, while a planted invariance failure fails R4 in every replicate. % R: analysis_brm/intermediate_panel/88_confusion.csv; analysis_brm/intermediate_panel/88_r4_steps.csv
A design that needs an R4 pass needs more personas per group than the corpus has.
