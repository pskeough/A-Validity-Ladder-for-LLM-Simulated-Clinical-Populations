\section{Controls: Real People and Planted Failures}
\label{sec:controls}

Every model fails every rung above the gate, so the ladder itself has to be shown to pass real people and to catch failures of a known size.
The controls build pseudo-models out of real survey respondents.
Each replicate splits the NHANES adults at random into two halves within the 48 persona cells, uses one half as the reference, and fills the corpus design from the other half, with 120 personas, two framings and 30 draws, where each draw is a real respondent from the persona's cell.
A panel of four such pseudo-models stands in for the four language models.
Failures are then planted into the same respondents, each aimed at one rung, by making the items independent, shifting every persona mean, doubling the income gap, compressing answer patterns toward the typical one, and making three items independent within the low-income group.
Run on the real corpus with the full reference, the control code reproduces all 303 published rung numbers. % R: 83a_receipt.csv

\paragraph{Real people pass, and planted failures are caught.}
Real respondents pass the gate and level 3 in every reading, level 1 in 92.9\% of replicates, and R1 and R2 of level 4 in every replicate, and level 2 fails them in no reading (Table~\ref{tab:panel}). % R: analysis_brm/intermediate_panel/88_confusion.csv
Each planted failure trips the rung it targets. % R: analysis_brm/intermediate_panel/88_confusion.csv
Independent items fail level 1, R1 and R2 in every replicate, shifted samples fail level 3 in every reading, compressed samples fail level 1 in every replicate, non-invariant samples fail R4 in all 25 replicates, and a doubled income gap fails level 2 in 92.5\% of per-model readings. % R: analysis_brm/intermediate_panel/88_confusion.csv
Two rungs also react to failures they do not target.
A sample shifted by 2.1 points passes the gate in only 28.6\% of readings, because more depressed respondents vary more between draws, and R2 fails compressed and non-invariant samples, because both change the size of the loadings. % R: analysis_brm/intermediate_panel/88_confusion.csv
Over a further 200 replicates with graded doses, the distortions the language models show at levels 1, 3 and 4 lie beyond the dose that each rung detects in 95\% of replicates (Supplement S5).
Under the persona design, real respondents' misfit ratio averages 1.15, and their misfit tail passes at $\tau = 1.5$ in 99.4\% of replicates, which sets the level-1 floor. % R: 83c_l1.csv

\paragraph{What level 2 and R4 need to certify a pass.}
Two rungs need more data to certify a pass than to detect a failure.
A second simulation repeated the panel over 60 replicates on grids of 48, 144 and 432 persona cells. % R: analysis_brm/panel_power/90_cells.csv; analysis_brm/panel_power/90b_summary.csv
Level 2 fails a faithful simulator in at most 3.3\% of per-model readings and fails a doubled income gap in 97.5\% to 100\%. % R: analysis_brm/panel_power/90b_summary.csv
At 30 draws per persona, a faithful simulator's low-to-high income gap is never read \vd{kept}, because the simulation's own error keeps its interval wider than the kept region. % R: analysis_brm/panel_power/power_l2_contrast_readings.csv
With no simulation error, which is the limit of adding draws, the same gap is read \vd{kept} in 95.0\% and 98.3\% of replicates on the 48-cell and the 144-cell grids. % R: analysis_brm/panel_power/power_l2_contrast_readings.csv; analysis_brm/panel_power/90_cells.csv
The Black--White and Hispanic--White gaps are never read \vd{kept} on any grid, because NHANES measures them too loosely. % R: analysis_brm/panel_power/power_l2_contrast_readings.csv
A researcher who needs a kept gap therefore plans for more draws per persona and for a population gap the survey measures precisely, and a finer persona grid does not supply the second.
R4 has the same property.
At the .08 margin, 24 to 40 personas per group leave some invariance steps unresolved for real respondents in 24 of 25 replicates, while a planted invariance failure fails R4 in every replicate. % R: analysis_brm/intermediate_panel/88_confusion.csv; analysis_brm/intermediate_panel/88_r4_steps.csv
A design that needs an R4 pass needs more personas per group than the corpus has.
