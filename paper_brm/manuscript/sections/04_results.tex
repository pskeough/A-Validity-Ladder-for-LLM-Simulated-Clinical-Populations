\subsection{Gate}

\input{tables/tab_gate}

No model may be read from a single draw (Table~\ref{tab:gate}).
The dependability of one draw runs from .21 for GPT-4o-mini under the narrative framing to .90 for Gemini-3-Flash. % R: gate_dstudy.csv
Gemini-3-Flash reaches .90 but its draws of one persona still spread with a standard deviation of about 1.5 points, above the 1-point limit. % R: gate_license_models.csv; gate_dstudy.csv
Persona means are readable at the corpus's 30 draws for every model under the minimum rule.
Under the recommended rule, the number of draws needed is 9 for DeepSeek-V3, 10 for Gemini-3-Flash, 21 for GLM-4.7 and 35 for GPT-4o-mini. % R: gate_license_models.csv
GPT-4o-mini's narrative persona means reach a dependability of .887 at 30 draws, short of .90. % R: gate_dstudy.csv
Levels 2 and 3 read the persona mean over both framings, 60 draws, and for that mean GPT-4o-mini reaches .967. % R: gate_license_level3.csv

Where dependability is low, the reason is that the model barely separates personas.
The draw-to-draw standard deviation is between 1.25 and 2.24 points in every model and framing. % R: gate_dstudy.csv
The variance between personas ranges from 0.81 for GPT-4o-mini (narrative) to 20.69 for Gemini-3-Flash (narrative), a 26-fold range. % R: gate_dstudy.csv
Setting the temperature to 0 in the decoding control cut GPT-4o-mini's draw variance to 12.5\% of its default value and did not raise the variance between personas. % R: gate_decoding_link.csv
At temperature 0 a single GPT-4o-mini draw reaches a dependability of only .75. % R: gate_decoding_link.csv
If a persona's score has to hold across the two prompt framings, DeepSeek-V3 and GPT-4o-mini cannot reach .90 with any number of draws (ceilings .83 and .88). % R: gate_dstudy_across_framing.csv

Two clinical draws of one persona fall in different PHQ-8 severity categories 35.2\% of the time [33.6, 36.8]. % R: gate_bandflip_summary.csv
This rate does not rank the models on stability.
A normal distribution with each cell's own mean and standard deviation, cut at the category boundaries, reproduces the cell flip rates with $R^2 = .88$, so flipping reflects how close a persona sits to a cut point as much as how much its draws vary. % R: gate_bandflip_regression.csv

The worked-case persona shows the same pattern.
Her 30 clinical draws run from 7 to 16 with a standard deviation of 2.45, and two of them fall in different severity categories 60.5\% of the time. % R: 85_worked_case.csv
Her first draw, 8, sits in the mild band, and her persona mean, 10.3, sits in the moderate band. % R: 85_worked_case.csv

\subsection{Level 1: Individual Coherence}

\input{tables/tab_l1}

No model passes level 1 in either framing (Table~\ref{tab:l1}).
At the same total score, model draws land in neither tail of the human person-fit distribution.
NHANES puts 5\% of adults below its within-total 5th percentile and 5\% above its 95th, and the models put 0.0\% to 1.0\% below and 0.0\% to 4.3\% above. % R: l1_summary.csv
DeepSeek-V3, Gemini-3-Flash and GPT-4o-mini are compressed in both framings, with too few atypical and too few highly regular answer patterns.
GLM-4.7 has the misfit deficit alone, because its interval for the highly regular tail lies inside the tolerance band in the narrative framing and crosses the band's lower edge in the clinical framing.
No verdict differs between the two framings, and every model fails at every tolerance from 1.10 to 3.00. % R: l1_personfit_verdicts_tau.csv
At the loosest tolerance, 3.00, Gemini-3-Flash keeps only its misfit deficit and clinical GPT-4o-mini only its overfit deficit. % R: l1_personfit_verdicts_tau.csv

The item profile shows where the typicality comes from.
At matched totals, the models score 0.16 to 0.51 points higher than people on depressed mood. % R: l1_personfit_item_profile.csv
They score 0.22 to 1.11 points lower on fatigue, one of the somatic items people endorse first. % R: l1_personfit_item_profile.csv
The models also repeat themselves across personas.
Two draws from different personas at the same total are identical vectors 4.6 to 39 times as often as two NHANES adults at that total. % R: l1_pattern_reuse.csv
On the gateway row, the models violate the PHQ-8's core-symptom rule at 0.5\% to 21\% of the human rate at the same totals. % R: l1_summary.csv

The worked-case persona's 60 draws sit wholly inside the human distribution.
None falls below the within-total 5th percentile, none falls above the 95th, and her mean probability integral transform is .40. % R: 85_worked_case.csv
None of her 32 elevated draws breaks the gateway rule. % R: 85_worked_case.csv
Each of her answers is a plausible human pattern, and together they are less varied than people at the same severity.

The verdicts hold on the December rows alone and under an unweighted calibration model. % R: l1_summary.csv
Against the smaller NHANES 2021--2023 sample, DeepSeek-V3 and GPT-4o-mini move from compressed to an overfit deficit, because their misfit tails become too imprecise to place (Supplement S3). % R: l1_personfit_main.csv

Figure~\ref{fig:level2} gives the per-model verdicts against the standardised NHANES gaps.
The gap between low and high income is steepened in every model, at 1.91 (DeepSeek-V3) to 5.07 (Gemini-3-Flash) times the population gap. % R: l2_headline.csv
Across the three income contrasts, 10 of the 12 per-model readings are steepened. % R: l2_headline.csv
The other two, Gemini-3-Flash's middle-to-high gap (1.31) and DeepSeek-V3's low-to-middle gap (1.33), read kept or steepened. % R: l2_headline.csv
On the marginal reference the income gaps are smaller relative to the population's, and Low minus High is still steepened for Gemini-3-Flash and GLM-4.7 and kept or steepened for the other two (Supplement S4). % R: l2_headline.csv

The sex gap divides the models.
GPT-4o-mini and DeepSeek-V3 keep a quarter to two fifths of the population's sex gap (ratios 0.23 and 0.41), read missing or attenuated. % R: l2_headline.csv
GLM-4.7 doubles it (2.06), and Gemini-3-Flash is undetermined. % R: l2_headline.csv
The pooled ratio of 0.97 averages these opposite errors. % R: l2_headline.csv

No racial contrast receives a single-region verdict in any model.
Among adults of equal income, sex and marital status, the NHANES Black--White gap is $-0.28$ points, while the crude gap is $+0.33$. % R: l2_reference.csv
The standardised Hispanic--White gap is $-0.18$, and its interval covers zero, so that contrast records no population gap for every model. % R: l2_stop.csv
For Black--White, GPT-4o-mini reads reversed or missing and the other three models are undetermined. % R: l2_headline.csv
For Asian--White, DeepSeek-V3 reads attenuated or kept and the other three are undetermined. % R: l2_headline.csv
The earlier report of these data read the Black--White and Hispanic--White gaps as missing \citep{keough2026plausible}.
That reading compared conditional simulated gaps with crude survey gaps, and it does not survive the like-for-like reference.

Two checks bear on these verdicts.
With the draw-only standard error, 9 of the 35 verdicts become sharper and none moves outside its primary verdict. % R: 83g_l2_conditional_verdicts.csv
Under four other mappings of relationship status in the reference, 18 of the 112 per-model verdicts change, 16 of them on race or sex, and the low-to-high income gap stays steepened in every model (Supplement S3). % R: l2_verdicts.csv
In the prompt control, the pooled low-to-high and low-to-middle income gaps stay steepened under all three system prompts, so the steepening does not come from the role or the population-reference instruction. % R: l2_prompt_control_verdicts.csv
With four persona pairs per model in that run, the per-model readings range from undetermined to steepened, and only Gemini-3-Flash's low-to-high gap is steepened under every prompt. % R: l2_prompt_control_verdicts.csv
The Controls section shows that at NHANES precision this rung can detect distortion and cannot award a faithful model the verdict \vd{kept}.

In the worked-case stratum (Black women who are single), GPT-4o-mini puts the low-income persona 3.4 points above the high-income one. % R: 85_worked_case.csv
In NHANES the same stratum differs by 1.6 points. % R: 85_worked_case.csv

\subsection{Level 3: Population Calibration}

Every model's simulated population sits above NHANES (Table~\ref{tab:l3}).
After post-stratification the overall residual is +2.1 points for GLM-4.7, +2.6 for Gemini-3-Flash, +4.6 for DeepSeek-V3 and +4.7 for GPT-4o-mini. % R: 80d_headline.csv
All 40 model-by-group residuals are positive, with 90\% intervals above zero. % R: 80d_pass_counts.csv; 80d_ranges.csv
No model passes all ten groups at 0.2 SD, 1 point or 2 points. % R: 80d_model_verdicts.csv
Of the 40 rows, none passes at 0.2 SD, one passes at 1 point, and six pass at 2 points. % R: 80d_pass_counts.csv
In every model the largest residual is in the low-income band. % R: 80c_l3_results.csv

Against NHANES 2021--2023, with cohabiting adults counted as married in both cycles, every residual falls by 0.9 to 2.1 points and no model passes all ten groups at 2 points. % R: 80d_era_shift_permodel.csv; 80d_model_verdicts_all.csv
The overall rows of Gemini-3-Flash and GLM-4.7 pass at 2 points against that cycle, so a use that needs only the national level would pass these two models against 2021--2023 and fail them against 2005--2018. % R: 80d_model_verdicts_all.csv
Across all eight declared reference specifications no model passes all ten groups at 2 points, and the most any model reaches is 8 of 10, for GLM-4.7 against 2021--2023. % R: 80c_l3_results.csv; 89_l3_spec_range.csv
The overall row passes at 2 points for GLM-4.7 under four specifications, the two persona-string income crosswalks and the 2017--2020 and 2021--2023 periods, and for Gemini-3-Flash under the 2021--2023 period alone. % R: 89_l3_spec_range_summary.csv
The model-level failure therefore holds under every specification, while an overall-row pass depends on the reference chosen.

The prevalence row does not follow the mean.
Pooled over models, 13.5\% of simulated respondents score 10 or more against 7.0\% in NHANES. % R: 80d_headline.csv
In the low-income band the simulated share is 55.5\% against 13.6\%. % R: 80d_headline.csv
In the middle and high income bands the simulated share is below the population's (4.4\% against 7.3\%, and 1.5\% against 3.7\%), although the means there are too high. % R: 80d_headline.csv
In 8 of the 50 primary rows (ten groups for each model and for the pooled population) the mean is significantly too high and the prevalence significantly too low. % R: 80d_sign_discordance_primary.csv
DeepSeek-V3's overall prevalence passes the ratio band (1.05) only because a low-income excess (32.7\% against 13.6\%) offsets shortfalls in the middle and high bands. % R: 80d_headline.csv; 80d_model_verdicts.csv
The worked-case persona's mean of 10.0 over both framings sits 5.3 points above her own NHANES cell (4.7). % R: 85_worked_case.csv
Against adults on Medicaid with household income under \$35,000, the label her prompt carried, the mean of all such adults is 5.5. % R: 85_worked_case.csv

\subsection{Level 4: Structural Fidelity}

\input{tables/tab_l4}

No model passes level 4 (Table~\ref{tab:l4}).
DeepSeek-V3 and GPT-4o-mini have no general factor in either framing.
Their ratios of the first to the second eigenvalue are 1.09 to 1.73, against 7.47 in NHANES, and each has negative loadings. % R: l4_structure.csv
The rule fails in every one of 1,000 persona bootstrap replicates for both models. % R: l4_structure.csv
Their PHQ-8 total is therefore not a one-dimensional score, and their later rules are description.

GLM-4.7 and Gemini-3-Flash pass R1 and R2.
Their congruence with the NHANES loadings is .990 to .998. % R: l4_structure.csv
An equal-loading vector scores .998 against the same loadings, so R2 adds little beyond R1 on this scale. % R: l4_nhanes_selfphi.csv
Both models fail R4 on income in both framings.
In NHANES, income is invariant at the .08 margin at both steps. % R: l4_invariance.csv
In the two models the metric step's RMSEA$_D$ is .16 to .20, with every lower 90\% limit above .13. % R: l4_invariance.csv
At the .08 margin, seven of the eight sex steps in these two models are indeterminate, because 24 to 30 personas per group cannot resolve them, and seven of the eight race steps hold, as they do in NHANES. % R: l4_invariance.csv; l4_invariance_pattern.csv
The verdicts are the same at the .05 margin. % R: l4_summary.csv

The R3 diagnostic shows where the factor in these two models comes from.
In NHANES, 3.5\% of item variance lies between demographic cells, and the covariance within cells carries the general factor on its own ($\lambda_1/\lambda_2 = 4.33$). % R: l4_between_within.csv
In Gemini-3-Flash and GLM-4.7, 48\% to 64\% of item variance lies between personas, and among draws of one persona there is no general factor ($\lambda_1/\lambda_2$ of 1.29 to 1.59). % R: l4_between_within.csv
The simulated correlation matrix is built from shifts in persona means, and draws of one persona share almost no common factor, unlike NHANES respondents within one demographic cell.
The same uniformity within personas appears at level 1, and it rules out the same single-draw uses.

The earlier report found that these data failed structural fidelity on income through a pooled congruence of .70 \citep{keough2026plausible}.
That value comes from pooling two models that have a general factor with two that do not. % R: l4_group_congruence.csv
Within each model that has a factor, the income congruence is .961 to .990, and the failure rests on the invariance test instead. % R: l4_group_congruence.csv
